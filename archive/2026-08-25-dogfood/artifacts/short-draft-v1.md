I spent months building the business case for two more operating rooms. I was wrong about which number was the constraint.

I sat down with a year of scheduling data because I wanted ammunition for the capital request. What I found was a Tuesday in February where six of our rooms sat idle at three in the afternoon while nine cases waited to get on the schedule.

We did not need rooms.

We needed to take block time away from four surgeons running under 60% utilization, and nobody wanted to be the person who did that. Including me. That is what the business case was for. A capital request is what you write when the real fix requires a conversation you do not want to have.

I withdrew it.

A room is the easiest thing in a hospital to count and the hardest thing to blame. It shows up in the capital plan, it has a square footage, and it never sits across from you in a block committee meeting and asks why its Thursday is gone.

Four surgeons under 60% has names attached to it. Two operating rooms does not.

That is the whole reason the second one is easier to ask for.
